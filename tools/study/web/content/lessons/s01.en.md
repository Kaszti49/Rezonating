## @bench
**2026-11-03, pc-10.** The boss asked: "Why does a *vibration* project read a *kitchen-scale* load cell, and can the HX711 go?" The answer was in the whole chain: the cell is a known test signal that runs through the same path a floor vibration will take, from the [[yzc133]] to the [[adc15]], the firmware, the link and the Lab.

By the end of this lesson you can walk that path box by box, say what each box counts or can lose, and answer the second question the boss will ask: why a new detection is a *pack* and not a new device.

## @intuition
### The node is an ear with a very strict secretary

Think of the node as a person listening to the floor.

- The **[[head|sensor head]]** is the ear. It turns shaking into a tiny electrical signal.
- The **[[adc|ADC]]** is the person who counts: thousands of times a second it writes down one number for the voltage.
- The **firmware** on the ESP32 is the secretary. It collects the numbers, cleans them up and, most important, **keeps a tally of every number it did not get**.
- The **[[link]]** is the phone line to the PC.
- The **Lab** and the **packs** are the brain. They decide "that was Anna's footstep".

Where the analogy breaks: an ear never knows what it missed. Our secretary must. A gap in the numbers shifts all timing and fools every model after it, so the rule is "count every sample".

### Order matters

Each box can only work with what the box before it gave. If something is lost early, nothing later can bring it back. The sharpest example is sampling too slowly:

### A pack is an app on the same phone

A phone does many jobs with the same hardware because apps are software. Our node works the same way. A [[pack]] says: "I need a geophone, 1 kSPS, this filter, this trigger, this model, and I report *person* and *step*". Footsteps, falls and machines are three packs on one node (D-002).

## @engineer
### The chain with our numbers

| Stage | What it does | Our values |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Floor | vibrates when someone steps | the footstep band is an expectation (roughly 10 Hz to a few hundred Hz), not yet measured by us |
| Head | vibration → volts | SM-24 geophone, 28.8 V/(m/s), arrives later; YZC-133 load cell as test signal; ADXL355 is digital |
| ADC | volts → 24-bit numbers | ADS131M02 on the ADC 15 Click, 2 channels, records at 2 kSPS (D-016) |
| Firmware | DRDY interrupt, FIFO, FIR low-pass, decimate to 1 kSPS, count losses | classic ESP32 DevKit V1 on the bench; ESP32-S3 planned, the choice is still open |
| Link | binary frames to the PC | one protocol over USB and Wi-Fi (D-018) |
| Lab / packs | WAV + sidecar, features, small model | `adc_raw` at 2 kSPS is what gets recorded (D-020) |

The ADS131M04 (4 channels) comes back on our own PCB. Until then the node has two analog channels, and the ADXL355 goes around the ADC on I²C.

### What a pack contains

A pack is a manifest (`pack.json`) plus a model. The footstep-id manifest in the repo, version 0.0.1, says:

| Field | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| head | geophone, 1 to 4 | which sensors it needs |
| sample rate | 1000 Hz | after the firmware decimation |
| DSP | high-pass 5 Hz, low-pass 400 Hz | keep only the useful band |
| trigger | STA/LTA, 50 ms and 2000 ms, ratio 3.5 | "something just happened" |
| window | 100 ms before, 400 ms after | the slice the features see |
| labels | P1, P2, P3, unknown | people are pseudonyms |

These are **starting values** in a version 0.0.1 manifest, not tuned results.

### What we are aiming at (targets, not results)

| Pack | Head | Year 1 target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| footstep-id | geophone | ≥ 85 % correct with 3 people |
| fall | geophone | ≥ 95 % of dummy falls, < 1 false alarm per week |
| appliance | accelerometer | flag an induced imbalance in 9 of 10 runs |

> [!ok] Software packs, not firmware changes, decide what a node detects. That is why a new idea costs a new pack and maybe a new head, not a new product.

### The ADC facts that shape everything else

- The ADS131M0x has no internal oscillator: it needs a continuous 8.192 MHz clock on CLKIN. On the Click an LTC6903 makes it, and the firmware must program it first. It is about 0.5 % typical accurate, so we measure the real sample rate and write it into the sidecar.
- All channels share one data rate (250 SPS to 64 kSPS).
- D-016: at 1 kSPS the ADC's own filter attenuates only −4.4 dB at the 500 Hz Nyquist frequency, so 500 to 800 Hz vibration would fold in nearly untouched. At 2 kSPS the 0 to 400 Hz band loses only −0.9 dB and the FIR cuts the rest before the step down to 1 kSPS. These are datasheet-based calculations from the decision note.
