## @bench
**The piezo question.** CLAUDE.md says the ADS131M0x inputs survive only AGND −1.6 V to AVDD +0.3 V, and that "piezo heads must be clamped and buffered". The SM-24 geophone and the load cell, on the other hand, go straight into the ADC 15 Click with no amplifier of ours: the load cell's 2 mV is read at gain 128. So when do you need an [[op-amp|op-amp]], and when does the chip's own [[pga|PGA]] do the job?

By the end you can derive the gain of the standard stages from two rules, and decide which head needs which.

## @intuition
### The op-amp is a patient referee

An op-amp has two inputs and one output. Inside it multiplies the *difference* between the inputs by an enormous number. On its own that is useless: any tiny difference slams the output into a rail. The trick is **negative feedback**: send part of the output back to the − input. Now the output moves only until the two inputs match, and then it rests. It is like a referee who keeps nudging until both sides are level.

That gives the two golden rules for an op-amp with negative feedback:

1. The output moves until the two inputs are at the **same voltage**.
2. The inputs draw **(almost) no current**.

{{predict:0}}

Where the picture breaks: the rules hold only while the output is not stuck at a rail, and only at frequencies where the op-amp still has gain to spare.

### The buffer: a copy that can push

Connect the output straight to the − input. The output must equal the + input, so gain is 1. It does not make the voltage bigger. It makes the signal **stronger**: the input draws no current, yet the output can drive a load. A weak source, like a piezo, stays untouched.

### Amplifiers: a voltage divider in the feedback

Put two resistors in the feedback path. The op-amp moves the output until the divider's middle point equals the + input. A bigger output is needed to reach it, and that is the gain.

{{w:opamp rf=47000 rg=1000 vin=0.05}}

Drag Vin up and watch the output: it follows ×48 until it hits the rail. Then it flattens. That is clipping.

### Single supply and rail-to-rail

We run on 3.3 V. The output cannot leave 0…3.3 V, and a classic op-amp stops 1–2 V short. [[rail-to-rail|Rail-to-rail]] types get close to both rails. For an AC signal on a single supply, the stage needs a mid-supply bias so both half-waves fit.

### Real inputs leak a little

Golden rule 2 is not exact. An [[input-bias|input bias current]] flows, and it makes a voltage across the source resistance. For a high-impedance source that matters.

{{calc:2}}

### Differential and instrumentation amplifiers

A **differential amplifier** (one op-amp, four resistors) amplifies V₊ − V₋ and rejects what is common to both ([[common-mode|common-mode]]). Its weakness: unequal source resistances unbalance it. An [[instrumentation-amp|instrumentation amplifier]] adds a buffer on each input (three op-amps), so the source resistance no longer matters, and one resistor sets the gain. This is what a load cell would need if we had no PGA. The [[cmrr|CMRR]] number tells how well the common part is rejected.

### What we do with each head

| Head | Signal | What we use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load cell YZC-133 | about 2 mV at 5 kg (calculated, 1 mV/V at about 2 V) | ADS131M02 PGA at gain 128, full scale ±9.375 mV. No op-amp. |
| SM-24 geophone | 28.8 V/(m/s) open-circuit | PGA at a lower gain, e.g. gain 32: ±37.5 mV (glossary); no op-amp |
| Piezo (later) | high impedance, spikes | clamp + buffer (op-amp, rail-to-rail) before the input |
| ADXL355 | digital | I²C, not the ADC |

Why the PGA saves us an op-amp: it is a low-noise differential amplifier built in, one per channel. The cost is that input limits apply (at gain ≥ 8 keep inputs between AGND −1.3 V and AVDD −1.8 V), which is why the bridge's supply needed the 690 Ω resistor in E02.

## @datasheet
### Op-amp datasheet, the lines that decide

| Parameter | Why it matters for us |
| --- | --- |
| Input bias current | high-impedance sources (piezo) need FET inputs with a tiny value |
| Input offset voltage | adds directly to a millivolt-sized signal |
| Gain-bandwidth product | the usable gain is GBW / frequency; our signals are below a few hundred Hz, so easy |
| Output swing (to the rails) | rail-to-rail or not, at a stated load |
| Input common-mode range | does it include the rails? |
| Supply range | must work from 3.3 V |

No part has been chosen for the piezo buffer. Choosing one is a decision, not part of this lesson.

### ADS131M02 / M04 datasheet

Look in **Electrical Characteristics** for input impedance, CMRR and the noise table per gain setting (we do not quote them; they were not in the repo), in **Absolute Maximum Ratings** for the input voltage limits above, and in the PGA description for the gain steps 1 to 128 and the ±1.2 V / gain full scale.
