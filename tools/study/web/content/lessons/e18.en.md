## @bench
**2026-11-01.** We counted DRDY pulses from the ADC 15 Click for 10 seconds: **1995.80 Hz** at OSR 2048. The ADC should give 2000. Working back, the clock on CLKIN is **≈ 8.1748 MHz**, not 8.192 MHz: **−0.21 %**. The firmware had asked the LTC6903 for 8.1915 MHz (word `0xCF60`). The chip delivered something else. By the end of this lesson you can tell what that error costs in samples per hour, and why the sidecar must carry the measured rate.

## @intuition
### A clock is a metronome, and the ADC counts its ticks

The ADS131M0x has no oscillator of its own. It needs a [[clkin|CLKIN]] signal: a steady stream of pulses, and every conversion is timed by counting them. If the metronome runs a little slow, every sample takes a little longer. The ADC still believes it is sampling at exactly 2000 per second. Only an outside timer can tell it isn't.

A [[crystal|quartz crystal]] is a slice of quartz that rings at a very stable frequency when an electric field pushes it, like a tuning fork. The QTM325 puts the crystal and its amplifier into one small can. The LTC6903 is different: it is an electronic oscillator, tuned by a number the firmware sends. It is flexible, but not as accurate.

{{predict:0}}

### % and ppm: two ways to say "a little off"

[[ppm]] is parts per million. 1 % is 10 000 ppm. A clock that is 100 ppm off loses 100 ticks in every million. The analogy of "a watch losing seconds" works: 2100 ppm is 7.56 seconds an hour.

### Jitter is a different kind of error

[[jitter|Jitter]] is how much single edges wobble around their ideal time. A clock can have a perfect average and still jitter, and the other way round. ppm hurts the *rate*; jitter hurts *noise*. We have not measured jitter on either clock.

### The numbers on our bench

$$ fDATA = fCLKIN / 2 / OSR $$

| Quantity | Value | Where from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OSR | 2048 | firmware config |
| Nominal CLKIN | 8.192 MHz | → 2000 SPS |
| LTC6903 word | 0xCF60 (OCT 12, DAC 984) | firmware; formula gives ≈ 8.1915 MHz |
| Measured DRDY | 1995.80 Hz, 10 s count | vault, 2026-11-01 |
| Measured CLKIN | ≈ 8.1748 MHz (−0.21 %) | calculated from the above |
| After the FIR (÷2) | ≈ 997.9 Hz | vault; Lab shows 997.3 to 997.96 Hz in different sessions |

{{calc:0}}

The firmware's chosen word aims at 8.1915 MHz because the LTC6903 steps in coarse increments and cannot make 8.192 MHz exactly. The remaining difference to the measured 8.1748 MHz is the chip's own error.

### What the error costs

{{w:clock err=-0.21}}

Use the presets: our LTC6903, its typical 0.5 %, its 1.1 % maximum and the QTM325.

{{calc:2}}

| Clock | Error | Samples off per hour at 1 kSPS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LTC6903, measured here | −0.21 % | 7 560 |
| LTC6903 typical | 0.5 % | 18 000 |
| LTC6903 max at 25 °C | 1.1 % | 39 600 |
| LTC6903 max over temperature | 1.65 % | 59 400 |
| QTM325 | ±25 ppm | 90 |

The LTC6903 also drifts: 10 ppm/°C and 0.05 %/V according to our part note (datasheet 69034fe). A reading taken at room temperature is therefore only right for that temperature and that supply.

### Why we measure and record the rate

- The ADC cannot tell. Only an external timer (the ESP32's) can compare DRDY pulses against real time.
- Frequencies are wrong by the same fraction: a 100 Hz feature sits at 99.79 Hz.
- Timestamps drift: 7.56 s per hour at −0.21 %.
- The recording sidecar should carry the measured rate, so any later analysis can correct. This is the rule in CLAUDE.md: measure the real sample rate and record it.

> [!ok] On the PCB with the QTM325 the error becomes tiny, but we still measure. 2000 × 25 ppm is ±0.05 Hz, not zero.

### Reading "accuracy" correctly

Three words appear in clock datasheets: **initial** accuracy (at the factory, at 25 °C), **temperature stability** (drift over the range) and **aging** (drift over years). The QTM325's ±25 ppm is a stability figure over −40…85 °C. Read the table notes for what the number includes. Look for the same split in the LTC6903 table before you compare the two on equal terms.

### Open point

The cheapest idea, to have the ESP32-S3 output 8.192 MHz itself, is in our part note as "inferred, jitter may hurt ADC noise, not tested". It is not a recommendation.
