## @bench
**2026-11-01, first bring-up.** The firmware programmed the clock chip, then read the ADC's ID register. Expected **0x2205**. It got **0x1102**, and register writes did nothing. DRDY was pulsing at about 1996 Hz, so the ADC was alive and converting. Adding `cs_ena_pretrans = 2` and `cs_ena_posttrans = 2` fixed it. Today you'll learn to see why from the bits alone, and to do the same for I²C and UART faults.

## @intuition
### SPI: a shared clock, and one line that says "you"

[[spi]] is four wires. The master drives the clock (SCLK) and the data out (MOSI). The device drives the data back (MISO). A fourth wire, [[chip-select|chip select]], says which device is being spoken to. Think of a classroom: the clock is a metronome, and chip select is the teacher saying a name. Everyone else keeps quiet.

The [[spi-mode|mode]] answers two questions: does the clock rest low or high ([[cpol]]), and does the receiver read on the first or the second edge ([[cpha]])? Both ends must agree. Our bus carries two devices with different answers: the LTC6903 clock chip uses mode 0 (CS2), the ADC uses mode 1 (CS1). Only the CS wire changes who listens.

{{w:spi bug=1}}

Tick and untick the bug box, and change the modes. With the bug on, the device starts talking one clock edge before the ESP32 is looking. Every bit is correct, but all land one place to the right.

{{predict:0}}

> [!warn] The analogy breaks on timing. A teacher saying a name and a student answering has no deadline. A chip select must be low for a minimum time *before* the first clock edge. That is the whole bug.

### I²C: two wires, addresses, and a polite pull

[[i2c]] has only SDA (data) and SCL (clock) for many devices. Each device has an address. The master starts with a [[start-stop|start condition]], sends 7 address bits and a read/write bit, and the addressed device answers with an [[ack|ACK]]. The lines are [[open-drain]]: devices only pull low, and a [[pull-up]] resistor lifts the line back up. That is why the rise time depends on R and C.

{{predict:1}}

### UART: no clock at all

[[uart]] has no clock wire. Both ends agree on a [[baud]] rate beforehand. Each byte is framed by a start bit (low) and a stop bit (high). If the stop bit isn't high where the receiver expects it, it flags a [[framing-error]]. Our link to the PC uses 115200 baud. Handoff note, 2026-11-02: about 0.07 % of frames are lost on this link. The cause is not documented yet.

### SPI on our bench

| Item | Value | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SCK / MISO / MOSI | GPIO 18 / 19 / 23 | firmware bring-up |
| CS1 (ADC), mode 1 | GPIO 17 | firmware |
| CS2 (LTC6903), mode 0 | GPIO 16 | firmware |
| ADC SCLK | 2 MHz | firmware (`ADC_SPI_HZ`) |
| LTC6903 SCLK | 1 MHz | firmware (`LTC_SPI_HZ`) |

One bit at 2 MHz lasts 500 ns. A [[setup-hold|CS setup]] problem that costs one clock edge is therefore a matter of hundreds of nanoseconds. That is why the symptom is so exact: not garbage, a clean right shift by one: `0x2205 >> 1 = 0x1102`.

> [!ok] The fix on the bench was `cs_ena_pretrans = 2` and `cs_ena_posttrans = 2` (ID read 0x2205 afterwards). A comment in the diagnostic table of the firmware also notes that on the classic ESP32 in full-duplex the pretrans setting may do nothing and that driving CS from a GPIO gives a real setup time. Why exactly the setting works is not settled in the repo. Say it that way.

The I²C limit for bus capacitance is 400 pF. A Cat5e cable has about 50 pF per metre (typical, from our cable note). 400 pF / 50 pF/m = **8 m**: our 20 m head cable would already exceed the limit with the cable alone. Whether heads get a mux per port with its own pull-ups, a bus extender, or something else is an open design question.

On the head side, all AT24C02 chips sit at 0x50 + A2..A0, and the TCA9548A mux is at 0x70–0x77: the MCU writes one byte to the mux to pick one of its 8 branches. That is the fix for the clash in the predict question.

### The logic analyser

A [[logic-analyser]] records several lines against one timeline and decodes them. For SPI, put one probe on each of CS, SCLK, MOSI and MISO, and a trigger on CS falling. Count clocks between CS low and the first SCLK edge: that is your setup time. For I²C, trigger on SDA falling while SCL is high (a start). It cannot show analogue problems like slow edges: use an oscilloscope for those.

### What the ADS131M02 frame looks like

The firmware reads frames of 12 bytes from the ADC (and 2 bytes for the LTC6903 word). The register map lists the ID register at address 0x00, reset value 22xxh. Read the command and response rules in the SPI section before you trust a single response word: the datasheet defines which frame carries the answer to which command.
