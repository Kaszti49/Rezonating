## @bench
**2026-11-01, first bring-up.** The firmware read the [[adc15]]'s ID register and expected **0x2205**. It got **0x1102**. The chip converted fine (DRDY at about 1996 Hz), but it ignored every register write. A day later, after the board was moved, the ID read **0xFFFF**.

Two different wrong answers, two different bus faults. By the end of this lesson you can tell them apart from the hex value alone, and you know which of our parts talks on which wire.

## @intuition
### Three ways to talk, three kinds of conversation

Think of three ways two people can talk.

- [[spi|SPI]] is a **walkie-talkie with a metronome**. One side (the master) clicks the clock; on every click, one bit goes out and one bit comes back. Four wires: clock (SCK), out (MOSI), in (MISO) and a "you, listen!" line per device ([[chip-select|CS]]). It is fast and simple, but every new device costs one more CS wire.
- [[i2c|I²C]] is a **meeting room with name tags**. Only two wires (SDA, SCL) for everyone. Each device has an address; the master says "0x53, please", and only that device answers. Slower, but cheap in wires. We use it for the [[adxl355]] and, later, the head EEPROMs.
- [[uart|UART]] is **two people with the same watch and no metronome**. No clock wire at all. Both agreed beforehand on the speed ([[baud|baud rate]]) and each byte starts with a start bit so the receiver can re-time. It is our link to the PC.

### SPI: one bus, two devices, two modes

On the ADC 15 Click the ESP32 talks to two chips over the same SCK, MOSI and MISO. The ADC (CS1) wants [[spi-mode|SPI mode]] 1. The LTC6903 clock chip (CS2) wants mode 0. The modes differ only in where the clock idles and on which edge a bit is read.

Now tick the "CS goes low too late" box in the diagram. The first clock edge is missed, and everything shifts right by one bit. That is our bench bug.

> [!warn] Where the analogy breaks: SPI has no "acknowledge". If the master talks to nothing, MISO just floats and it reads all ones or all zeros. That is the 0xFFFF story: a loose jumper, not a broken chip.

### I²C: why the resistors

I²C wires are [[open-drain]]: a device can only pull a wire down. Something has to bring it back up. That is the [[pull-up]] resistor.

### UART: counting what gets lost

Our link to the PC is a UART at 115200. The firmware gives every frame a sequence number. If one number is missing, the PC knows a frame was lost and counts it. On 2026-11-02 the loss was about **0.07 %** of frames. The cause is not diagnosed in our notes; a higher baud rate is listed as an idea in the Handoff. S07 shows how the counting works.

The pin map for the ESP32-S3 is **not decided**; a proposal is shown in the Build view.

Setup time matters. The ADC wants CS low for a minimum time before the first SCK edge. At 2 MHz one clock period is 500 ns. With ESP-IDF's default CS timing the ADC was not ready, started one bit late, and every word came back halved:

$$ 0x2205 = 8709  →  8709 / 2 = 4354 = 0x1102 $$

{{calc:0}}

The fix is `cs_ena_pretrans = 2` and `cs_ena_posttrans = 2` (counted in SPI clock cycles), or driving CS from a plain GPIO. Changing the mode or the MISO delay did not help, as the fault case shows.

### I²C: rise time

The time constant of the pull-up and the bus capacitance sets the edge speed:

$$ t_r(30 % → 70 %) = 0.8473 · R · C $$

{{calc:2}}

| Mode | Clock | Max rise time (I²C standard) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Standard | 100 kHz | 1000 ns |
| Fast | 400 kHz | 300 ns |

The ADXL355 runs in I²C mode with SCLK/VSSIO tied to GND, address 0x1D (ASEL low) or 0x53. Whether its breakout board already has pull-ups is not known. Each [[at24c02]] head EEPROM would sit at 0x50, so identical heads clash; that is the job of the [[tca9548a]] mux.

### What the limits mean for us

- The ADC's setup time before the first SCK edge is the number our bug violated. We did not find a single datasheet value that explains the exact timing of ESP-IDF's default; the fix was found by experiment (the diagnostic table in `bringup_main.c`), not derived.
- The ADS131M02's mode is 1. Our code notes that mode 0 is outside the specification and marginal.
- The ADXL355 pin text says to connect SCLK/VSSIO to VSSIO for I²C. Until the breakout is checked, assume nothing about its pull-ups.
