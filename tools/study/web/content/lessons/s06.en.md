**2026-11-03.** The lab photos from 2026-10-13 showed that the board on the bench is a **classic ESP32** ([[esp32-devkit]]), not the [[esp32-s3|ESP32-S3]] we planned the node around. It still reads the ADC, streams about 997 Hz and counts its losses. The Handoff asks Kaszti and Viktor: "Is the classic ESP32 the node now?"

That question is **open**. This lesson doesn't answer it. It gives you the facts so that either answer comes with numbers.

## @intuition
### What a microcontroller is made of

A microcontroller ([[firmware|firmware]] runs on it) is a small computer on one chip. Think of a tiny workshop.

- **Cores** are the workers. Both of our chips have two, running at up to 240 MHz. We give one the time-critical job (answering the ADC's DRDY signal) and the other the slower job (talking to the PC).
- **RAM** is the workbench: fast, small, forgotten at power-off.
- **Flash** is the cupboard: the program lives there and survives power-off.
- **[[psram|PSRAM]]** is a big table out in the corridor: much more space, a bit slower to reach. The S3 N16R8 has 8 MB of it. The bench board has none.
- **[[gpio|GPIO]] pins** are the doors. Not every door is free: some lead into the flash or the PSRAM, some are checked at the moment the workshop opens in the morning.

### The doors that bite

At reset, a few pins are read to choose boot settings. They are [[strapping-pin|strapping pins]]. If something on the wire pulls one to the wrong level at that moment, the board won't start.

> [!warn] Where the analogy breaks: after boot a strapping pin is a normal GPIO. The danger is only at reset. That is why the fault looks random: it depends on what is attached at power-up.

### Why a buffer matters

The ADC never waits for the PC. If the link is slow for a moment, samples pile up in a queue. When the queue is full, new samples are dropped and counted. More memory means more time to recover.

## @engineer
### The two boards side by side

| | Bench: ESP32 DevKit V1 | Planned: ESP32-S3-DevKitC-1 N16R8 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Chip | classic ESP32, WROOM-32 module | ESP32-S3 |
| Cores | 2 (up to 240 MHz) | 2 (up to 240 MHz) |
| Flash | 4 MB (boot log) | 16 MB |
| PSRAM | none | 8 MB octal |
| USB to PC | CP2102 bridge (UART) | native USB, plus a bridge port |
| ML help | none that we use | vector instructions (the part note's reason for choosing it) |
| Taken GPIOs | 6–11 (flash) | 33–37 (PSRAM), 19/20 (USB) |
| Input-only | 34, 35, 36, 39 | check the datasheet |
| Strapping | 0, 2, 5, 12, 15 | check the datasheet |
| In the lab? | yes | marked `needed` in the vault |
| Pin map | bench pins: SCK 18, MISO 19, MOSI 23, CS1 17, CS2 16, DRDY 4, RST 27 | **not decided**; a proposal is shown in the Build view |

The link protocol was written for the S3's native USB (the port reports VID:PID 303A:1001). On the bench the same bytes travel over the UART at 115200. That is a difference in speed, not in format.

The bench has about 22 kB of queue; a node with PSRAM could hold about 10 s of the full 2 kSPS stream. That difference decides how long a USB or Wi-Fi hiccup can last before samples are lost.

### The two sides, fairly

**For keeping the classic ESP32 as the node**
- It is here, it works, and 997 Hz streaming with zero device drops is already measured on it.
- No new pin map and no re-test of the SPI timing fix.
- Nothing to order or wait for.

**For moving to the ESP32-S3**
- PSRAM for large buffers and for the recordings pipeline on the node.
- Vector instructions that suit the small ML model (TFLite Micro, S08).
- Native USB, and the protocol is written for it.
- The firmware README and the first order (ORD-001) already assume it.

**Not known yet**
- Whether the S3 boards are in the lab (the part note says `needed`).
- Whether the footstep-id model will fit and run in time on the classic chip: not measured.
- The S3 SPI behaviour with our chip-select fix: not tested.

This is a decision for Kaszti and Viktor; write it in a decision note before changing the firmware target.

### What the limits mean for us

- A pin in the PSRAM range is not "probably free". It is gone, and the board may misbehave in strange ways if you use it.
- GPIO 12 on the classic chip is a flash-voltage setting. Our part note forbids a pull-up there.
- The S3 numbers in this lesson for flash and PSRAM come from the part note. Strapping and input-only pins on the S3 are not in our notes: read the datasheet before designing.
