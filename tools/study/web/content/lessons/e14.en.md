## @intuition
### Noise is what the world adds to your signal

Think of listening to a quiet voice in a room. Some noise is in the room however you arrange it (people breathing). Some comes in through the door (traffic). Some is your own ears.

- **Thermal noise:** every resistor jitters a little, because its charge carriers are warm. Cannot be removed, only reduced by lowering the resistance, the bandwidth or the temperature.
- **Quantization noise:** the ADC rounds the voltage to the nearest step. That error behaves like noise.
- **1/f (flicker) noise:** slow drifts and wanders. Stronger the lower the frequency; a worry for DC measurements, much less for vibration above a few Hz.
- **Interference:** signals from outside: 50 Hz mains hum, a switching supply, the ESP32's Wi-Fi, a knock on a wire. This is the one you can fight with wiring.

Where the room picture breaks: unlike a person, the ADC cannot tell voice from noise. Anything that arrives on the two input wires is signal.

### Wires are antennas, loops catch fields

A loop of wire catches magnetic fields like a coil. A long wire catches electric fields like an antenna. The cure comes in three layers:

1. **Make the loop small:** run signal and return together. A twisted pair is the extreme case, and it also makes both wires pick up the *same* disturbance.
2. **Measure the difference:** a [[differential|differential input]] subtracts the two wires, so the shared disturbance cancels.
3. **Block the field:** a metal [[shield]] around the cable, connected to ground at one end.

{{predict:1}}

> [!warn] A differential input rejects only the *common* part. A disturbance that hits one wire more than the other (a bad contact on one wire, for instance) is seen in full.

### Grounds

[[ground|Ground]] is a reference, not a perfect 0 V everywhere. A [[ground-loop|ground loop]] forms when two paths connect the same two points; the loop picks up fields, and currents in the ground wire make voltages. [[star-ground|Star ground]] fixes it: every return meets at one point. On a mixed-signal board, [[agnd-dgnd|AGND and DGND]] are kept apart and joined at one point so digital switching currents do not flow through the quiet analog reference. On the ADC 15 Click the board already makes this choice; on our own ADS131M04 board we have to make it.

### Our Cat5e head cable

The head port is RJ45, labelled "not Ethernet". Plan (Master plan): pins 1–2 analog ±, 3–6 I²C, 4–5 +3.3 V, 7–8 GND. In standard Cat5e wiring each of these is one [[twisted-pair|twisted pair]]. So the analog signal travels on a pair, and the node reads it **differentially** (AINxP − AINxN). I²C and the supply are on their own pairs, away from the analog one.

### What the CH2-on-GND channel tells you

Both inputs of CH2 sit on GND, so the channel shows the ADC's own noise and offset, plus whatever couples in near the inputs.

{{predict:0}}

Three outcomes:

| CH2 does this during handling | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Stays quiet while CH1 spikes | the disturbance is on CH1's own path: wires, contacts, the cell |
| Moves together with CH1 | something shared: ground, supply, the clock or board, pickup near both inputs |
| Quiet, and CH1 also quiet | the rig is fine |

The Handoff says CH1 and CH2 moved together earlier. That points away from the cell, but it is one observation, not a diagnosis. The suspect in the build note is a loose green or white contact; the wiggle test is still to be done.

## @datasheet
### ADS131M02 / M04

- **Electrical Characteristics:** the input-referred noise per gain and data rate, CMRR, input impedance. Read the noise table at **gain 128 and OSR 2048**, our setting. We do not quote the values here.
- **Absolute Maximum Ratings:** the input voltage range limits already in CLAUDE.md.
- **Layout guidelines / Application information:** the AGND/DGND advice, decoupling of AVDD and DVDD, and what to do near the inputs. Follow it when we draw our PCB.

### Cat5e

Category 5e cable has four twisted pairs; the pair assignment above is the common wiring standard. Our use of the pairs for analog, I²C and power is project-specific. Cable length and cable capacitance matter for I²C (E12) and for noise pickup; we have not measured either yet.

### What no datasheet tells you

Pickup depends on your wiring. Only a measurement on the rig shows it: record CH2 with and without handling, and with a short and a long cable, and keep the results in the build note.
