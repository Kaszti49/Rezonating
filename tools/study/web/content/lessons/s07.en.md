## @bench
**2026-11-02, link mode on the bench.** The site streams the real board: `adc_dec` at 997.3 Hz (−0.21 % from the 1000 Hz nominal), zero device drops. The Handoff also says: "link loss at 115200: about 0.07 % of frames, plus 200 host-side samples."

Someone has to count those losses, and the counter has to be honest. This lesson follows one sample through the firmware, shows every place it can disappear, and shows how each place is counted.

## @intuition
### The path of a sample

Picture a conveyor belt in a bakery.

1. The ADC bakes a loaf every 0.5 ms and rings a bell: the [[drdy|DRDY]] pin goes low.
2. The bell is an [[interrupt|interrupt]]. A tiny handler writes down the time and wakes the baker. It does nothing else: the next bell could ring soon.
3. The baker (a task) collects the loaf from the ADC over [[spi|SPI]]. The ADC has room for two loaves per channel (a small [[fifo]]), so a late baker can still collect both.
4. A sieve (the [[fir|FIR filter]]) smooths the loaves, and the packer keeps every second one ([[decimation]]): 2 kSPS in, 1 kSPS out.
5. The packer puts 40 loaves in a box with a number on it (a frame) and puts the box on the belt to the PC: the [[uart|UART]] link.

{{w:decimate f=700 taps=63}}

> [!warn] Where the analogy breaks: a real bakery just makes more bread if a loaf is missed. Here a missed loaf is a hole in the data, and we write down exactly where the hole is.

### Where a sample can be lost

{{predict:0}}

There are five places, and each one is counted:

- the bell was not heard (a **missed DRDY edge**);
- the baker was too late and the ADC's mailbox overwrote a loaf (a **FIFO overflow**);
- the loaf arrived damaged (a **[[crc|CRC]] error**);
- the belt to the PC was full (the **send queue**);
- the PC lost a box on the way (a **gap in seq numbers**).

### Why nothing may be hidden

If the firmware quietly filled holes with plausible numbers, a later step could mistake a hole for a footstep. A lost sample that looks like data teaches the model something false. So a hole is always written down as a hole.

## @engineer
### The interrupt handler, from the real firmware

{{predict:1}}

```c
static void IRAM_ATTR drdy_isr(void *arg)
{
    uint32_t e = s_drdy_edges;
    s_drdy_t[e & (DRDY_T_RING - 1)] = esp_timer_get_time();
    s_drdy_edges = e + 1;
    BaseType_t hp = pdFALSE;
    vTaskNotifyGiveFromISR(s_adc_task, &hp);
    if (hp == pdTRUE) portYIELD_FROM_ISR();
}
```

It stores a microsecond timestamp in a ring, counts the edge, and wakes the ADC task (pinned to core 1, priority 10). The link task runs on core 0, so a slow link never delays the ADC. A full packet goes into a queue with a zero timeout: if the queue is full the packet is dropped and counted, never waited for.

### The FIFO and the narrow pulse

The ADS131M02 keeps two samples per channel. After the first of two queued frames is read, DRDY gives a narrow extra pulse. The firmware has to tell these from real conversion edges (`rz_drdy.c`). The rule: conversion edges sit one data period T apart. With gap g between edges and m = round(g / T):

- m = 1: a conversion;
- m ≥ 2: a conversion after m − 1 skipped intervals;
- anything else: the narrow pulse.

{{calc:2}}

Left-over frames in the FIFO are found the same way: a pulse nobody explains means one frame is still waiting, so the firmware reads it and drops it.

### The decimator

```c
bool rz_decim_push(rz_decim_t *d, const int32_t *x, bool valid, float *y, bool *out_valid);
```

63 taps, linear phase, delay (63 − 1) / 2 = 31 raw samples. A lost raw sample is replaced by the previous good one, and `lost_until` remembers that the next 63 outputs touch it. An output at raw index n is valid only if n ≥ `lost_until`. The firmware is a C port of the Python decimator (`rezonating_ml.dsp`), checked against shared test vectors, and built with `-ffp-contract=off` so both give the same float results.

### The link frame

```
raw  = header(ver, type, seq) | payload | crc16     (CRC-16/CCITT-FALSE)
wire = COBS(raw) | 0x00
```

[[cobs|COBS]] removes every 0x00 from the frame body, so the 0x00 delimiter can only mean "frame end".

The firmware sends 25 DATA frames per second of 40 sample frames each.

About 76 % of the 115200 link, calculated. The bench link has to stay under 100 %, which is why the bench streams only `adc_dec`; `adc_raw` at 2 kSPS would need more than the link has.

### The counters that stop a hidden loss

Every STATUS frame (once per second) reports, per stream: `produced`, `device_dropped`, `rate_mhz`, `clipped`, and the sending-window counts `win_device` and `win_link`. The PC adds the third count, the host gaps it sees in `first_sample`. The sidecar stores `loss.device`, `loss.link`, `loss.host`.

On the real run: link loss about 0.07 % of frames. If that rate applied to the 25 DATA frames per second, it would be about 1 frame per minute. The cause isn't diagnosed in our notes.

{{w:clock err=-0.21}}

The clock error matters for another reason. The LTC6903 clock is −0.21 % off, so a "1000 Hz" stream is really 997.3 Hz. The firmware measures the real rate and the sidecar records it.

### What the limits mean for us

- The DRDY classifier tolerates interrupt latency up to about 100 µs in host simulation. At 130 µs and more the sample index can shift by one for good, and nothing reports it yet (an open low-severity flag). So latency on core 1 must stay well under that.
- The ADC's FIFO holds two samples: a reader that is more than about two periods late loses data.
- PROTOCOL.md rules: a lost sample is never sent, not even as a placeholder; "Every sample interval gets the next index, even when its sample is lost."
