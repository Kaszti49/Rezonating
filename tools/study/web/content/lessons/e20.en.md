## @intuition
### A PCB is the schematic, made permanent

Think of the schematic as a recipe and the PCB as the finished dish. The recipe says "salt, then pepper". The dish has to put them on a plate, where position, order and mess matter. On a PCB, copper tracks are the wires, and they have resistance, inductance and capacitance that your schematic ignores. At 8.192 MHz, with microvolt signals, that is enough to matter.

### The flow in five steps

1. **Schematic** (in [[kicad|KiCad]]): parts and connections. Add [[reference-designator|reference designators]] and [[net-label|net labels]].
2. **Footprints:** each part gets its [[footprint]], the copper pads the part is soldered to. A wrong footprint is a dead board.
3. **Layout:** place parts, then route tracks.
4. **[[drc|DRC]]:** the program checks the layout against the fab's limits.
5. **[[gerber|Gerbers]]:** the files the fab reads. You send them, with the drill file, to get boards back.

### Why the ground plane is a big deal

Every signal current has to flow back to its source. This is the [[return-path|return path]]. A [[ground-plane|ground plane]] (a whole layer of copper at GND) gives each signal a short way home right under its track. Cut the plane with long slots, and the return loop grows and picks up noise. The analogy to a river breaks here: current doesn't just flow downhill, it always takes the lowest-impedance route, and that is directly under the track at high frequency.

## @engineer
### The layout rules that matter for the ADS131M04

These are standard mixed-signal practices. The exact placement rules for the ADS131M04 come from its datasheet's layout section: read it before you draw. This is a method, not our finished design.

| Rule | Why |
| --- | --- |
| One solid ground plane, no cuts under the ADC | short return paths; no loops |
| [[decoupling|Decoupling capacitor]] at each supply pin (AVDD, DVDD), same side, short path to ground via | spikes are served locally |
| CAP pin: 220 nF to DGND, close to the pin | the internal digital LDO needs it (from the pin table) |
| QTM325 right next to CLKIN, short track, ground under it, its own decoupling | the clock is the fastest edge on the board (see below) |
| Analog inputs routed as a pair, away from SPI and CLKIN | digital edges (3.3 V in ns) couple into microvolt signals |
| SPI and DRDY tracks on the digital side of the ADC | keeps their return currents away from the analog side |

The ADC pins fall roughly into an analog side (AIN0–AIN3, AVDD, AGND) and a digital side (SPI, DRDY, CLKIN, DVDD, DGND). Use that to place the ADC so that the analog inputs face the head ports and the digital pins face the MCU.

> [!ok] On our PCB the LTC6903 disappears (the QTM325 replaces it), so CS2 is gone. The SPI bus then carries only the ADC, plus possibly the SD card. SD cards in SPI mode are normally used with mode 0, and the ADC needs mode 1: the mixed-mode issue from E16 returns if they share a bus. The README leaves "a second bus or a separate CS" open.

### Head ports

The Master plan gives the draft pinout (not final): RJ45 pins 1–2 analog ±, 3–6 I²C, 4–5 +3.3 V, 7–8 GND. T568B pairs: 1–2 orange, 3–6 green, 4–5 blue, 7–8 brown. Label every port "not Ethernet".

- The **analog pair** (1–2) is one twisted pair, so noise hits both wires equally and the differential input cancels it.
- **Input protection** goes right at the connector: series resistor and clamp (E11), because a head, or the wrong plug, can put anything on those pins. The Master plan says the piezo clamp is mandatory.
- **I²C** on pins 3–6 reaches the head's AT24C02 ([[eeprom]], head descriptor) and, for the accelerometer head, the ADXL355.
- Four ports share the same I²C pins at the node, and every head EEPROM is at 0x50. The plan: a [[mux|TCA9548A mux]] (0x70–0x77) at the node, one branch per port. The MCU writes one byte to pick the branch. Check the datasheet for the exact meaning of the byte.

### Pin map for the MCU

A proposal exists for the ESP32-S3 node (not decided): SCK 12 · MISO 13 · MOSI 11 · CS1 10 · CS2 6 · DRDY 4 · RST 5 · SDA 8 · SCL 9 · SD CS 14. GPIO 33–37 are off limits (octal PSRAM). Strapping pins on the S3 are 0, 3, 45, 46. A strapping pin held by a pull-up or a connected device can stop the board from booting.

### Footprints are a datasheet job too

Footprint names in our parts data are guides: TSSOP-20 body 6.5 × 4.4 mm, 0.65 mm pitch for the ADS131M04; 3.2 × 2.5 mm, 4 pads for the QTM325. Compare each footprint with the *package drawing* in the datasheet, pad by pad, before you order. A DRC pass does not catch a wrong footprint.

### Ordering from a fab

Before you send Gerbers: run DRC with the fab's own rules (minimum track, spacing, drill), look at the Gerbers in a viewer, check the board outline and the silkscreen, and read the fab's own checklist. Which fab and how many boards is a purchase question: it goes to Kaszti first.
