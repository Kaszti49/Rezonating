## @bench
**2026-11-03, pc-10.** With the [[yzc133]] on the [[adc15]] at gain 128, channel 0 rested at about −20 µV. Pressing the cell stepped it to +400 … +900 µV. But while the cell was being handled, the log also showed spikes of −3 to −4.5 mV and at least one reading of **−9375 µV**, the very end of the range. And on 2026-11-01 we had measured the ADC's sample rate at 1995.80 Hz instead of 2000.

What do those numbers actually mean? By the end of this lesson you can turn any voltage into a code and back, tell a clipped sample from a real one, and see why the sample rate is a measured number and not a setting.

## @intuition
### The ADC is a ruler with 16.7 million marks

A 24-bit [[adc|ADC]] reads a voltage against a ruler with 2²⁴ = 16 777 216 marks. The ruler spans from −full scale to +full scale. A reading is "which mark is the voltage closest to". The spacing between marks is one [[lsb|LSB]].

If the ruler is long (±1.2 V), each mark is a large step. If you shorten it (±9.375 mV), the marks pack closer: a small signal then spans many marks. That is what the [[pga|PGA]] does. It is a zoom lens in front of the ruler. The [[full-scale|full scale]] is ±1.2 V divided by the gain.

> [!warn] Zoom has a cost. Zoom in 128× and the ruler is only ±9.375 mV long. A bigger signal does not fit: it sticks at the end mark ([[clipping]]). The −9375 µV on our bench is exactly that end mark.

### The clock is a metronome

The ADC measures once per tick of its metronome. The ADS131M0x has **no** metronome of its own, so we supply one on the [[clkin|CLKIN]] pin: 8.192 MHz, continuous. On the ADC 15 Click that comes from a small chip, the LTC6903, which the ESP32 first has to *program* to 8.192 MHz. It is a cheap metronome: a little slow or a little fast.

### The doorbell

When a new number is ready, the ADC pulls the [[drdy|DRDY]] pin low, like a doorbell. The ESP32 hears it, walks over and reads the number over [[spi]]. Every ring should lead to exactly one number. A ring without a read, or a read without a ring, is a lost sample, and the firmware must count it.

### The clock sets the sample rate

$$ f_data = CLKIN / (2 · OSR) $$

With CLKIN = 8.192 MHz and OSR 2048 the rate is 2000 SPS (D-016). The firmware programs the LTC6903 (word 0xCF60 in our bring-up) and counts DRDY edges against the ESP32 timer for 10 s: 1995.80 Hz, so CLKIN ≈ 8.1748 MHz, **−0.21 %**. That was at room temperature on one board. The LTC6903 is specified at 0.5 % typical, 1.1 % max (at 25 °C) and up to 1.65 % over temperature, so the number can differ tomorrow.

### One sample, end to end

1. The ADC finishes a conversion and pulls **DRDY low** (falling edge).
2. The ESP32 interrupt fires and signals a task.
3. The task reads one frame over **SPI mode 1**. The frame holds a status/response word, one word per channel and a CRC word (check the datasheet for the exact layout). Chip-select needs extra setup and hold time: with ESP-IDF's default every word came out shifted by one bit, and `cs_ena_pretrans`/`cs_ena_posttrans` = 2 fixed it.
4. A bad CRC counts as a lost sample.
5. The 24-bit word is sign-extended to int32.
6. It goes into a FIFO. A gap (DRDY edges missing, FIFO left-overs) is counted.
7. The firmware FIR (pass band to 400 Hz) and decimation by 2 produce the 1 kSPS `adc_dec` stream. The 2 kSPS `adc_raw` stream goes out as it is.
8. The link sends `DATA` frames with a 64-bit first-sample index and a device timestamp, plus a 1 Hz `STATUS` with drops and the measured rate.

Noise: the datasheet gives input-referred noise by gain and data rate. We do not quote a number here: check the datasheet table.

The ADS131M04's pin table is verified in the vault (TSSOP-20): 1 AVDD, 2 AGND, 3 to 10 the four differential input pairs, 11 SYNC/RESET, 12 CS, 13 DRDY, 14 SCLK, 15 DOUT, 16 DIN, 17 CLKIN, 18 CAP, 19 DGND, 20 DVDD. The M02 pin table has not been fetched.

### Why 2 kSPS (D-016, datasheet-based calculations)

- At 1 kSPS the ADC's own sinc filter attenuates only −4.4 dB at 500 Hz, so 500 to 800 Hz vibration folds in almost unattenuated.
- At 2 kSPS the 0 to 400 Hz band loses only −0.9 dB, and everything that could fold into it (1600 to 2400 Hz) is cut by at least 17.5 dB. The FIR then removes 400 to 1000 Hz before decimation.

### LTC6903 (Analog Devices)

Programmed over SPI mode 0 (CS2 = mikroBUS PWM pin). Accuracy 0.5 % typical, 1.1 % max at 25 °C and 2.7 V, up to 1.65 % over temperature (C grade); 10 ppm/°C; 0.05 %/V. It powers up at 1.039 kHz until programmed.

### The ADC 15 Click board

Two 2-pole screw terminals: CH1 (− left, + right), CH2 (+ left, − right). No excitation or ground terminal. Left header: NC, RST, CS1, SCK, SDO, SDI, 3V3, GND. Right header: CS2, INT (= DRDY), NC ×5, GND. 3.3 V logic only; no 5 V pin is connected. Screw CH1 = ADC channel 0, confirmed by the press test.
