## @intuition
### Four heads, four tricks

Every head turns a mechanical thing into an electrical thing. The trick differs.

- **Geophone ([[geophone]], [[sm24]]).** A magnet hangs on springs inside a coil. When the floor moves, the case moves with it, but the magnet's inertia keeps it behind. A coil moving through a magnetic field produces voltage, so the output is proportional to how *fast* they move relative to each other: **velocity**. No motion, no voltage.
- **Load cell ([[load-cell]], [[yzc133]]).** A metal beam with four thin foil [[strain-gauge|strain gauges]] glued on. Press the beam and it bends a hair; two gauges stretch, two compress, their resistance changes by a tiny fraction. Wired as a [[wheatstone-bridge|bridge]] it gives millivolts. It measures **force**, and it needs a supply voltage ([[excitation]]).
- **Accelerometer ([[accelerometer]], [[adxl355]]).** A speck of silicon on silicon springs inside a chip ([[mems]]). The chip senses how far the speck lags and converts that to a number itself. It measures **acceleration** (and gravity!), and it talks digitally over [[i2c]].
- **Piezo** (later). A crystal that makes a charge when squeezed. Very sensitive, very high output impedance, can make voltage spikes. It will need a clamp and a buffer in front of the ADC.

Where the analogy breaks: the geophone is passive (no power needed), the load cell needs power but is analog, the accelerometer needs power and is digital. Only the first two go through the ADC.

### The geophone has a favourite frequency

A mass on a spring rings at its [[natural-frequency|natural frequency]]: 10 Hz for the SM-24. Above that it follows the floor nicely; near it, it exaggerates unless it is [[damping|damped]]; below it, it responds less and less. Damping is how fast the ringing dies. Try it:

{{w:geophone zeta=0.6 f=10 vel=1}}

Move the frequency below 10 Hz and the output shrinks. Change the damping and watch the peak near 10 Hz flatten or grow. That is one reason the geophone suits a floor band that starts around 10 Hz and not at DC.

### A bridge is a balance scale

Four resistances in a diamond. At rest both halves split the voltage the same, so the two middle points sit at the same voltage and the difference is zero. Press the beam and the halves split differently; the *difference* is the signal.

## @engineer
### Geophone: volts from velocity

$$ V = 28.8 V/(m/s) × v $$

| Floor velocity | Output |
| --- | --- |
| 10 µm/s | 288 µV |
| 100 µm/s | 2.88 mV |
| 1 mm/s | 28.8 mV |

{{calc:0}}

The ADC input range is ±1.2 V divided by the gain. The master plan has the geophone straight into the ADC at gain 32 to 64:

{{calc:1}}

The geophone's coil is a source with a resistance of a few hundred ohms and a mechanical damping that depends on what loads it. Secondary sources give a natural frequency of 10 Hz ±2.5 %, a coil of about 375 Ω ±2.5 %, and damping about 0.25 with the coil open, about 0.6 with a 1339 Ω shunt. We have not measured these and the datasheet is not in our repo: treat them as to-be-confirmed.

### Load cell: millivolts from force

The output is proportional to excitation, rating and load fraction:

$$ V_out = (1 mV/V) × V_exc × (load / 5 kg) $$

With the 690 Ω series resistor the bridge sees about 2.03 V (calculated, 2.01 to 2.04 V):

| Load | Output |
| --- | --- |
| 1 kg | 0.41 mV |
| 2 kg | 0.81 mV |
| 5 kg | 2.03 mV |

{{calc:2}}

The full 5 kg output of 2.03 mV fits inside ±9.375 mV at gain 128. Remember why the resistor is there: the bridge's sense lines sit at half the supply, and at gain 128 an ADS131M02 input must stay below about AVDD −1.8 V.

### Accelerometer: numbers directly

The ADXL355 gives acceleration as a number; it needs no gain setting on the ADC. It runs from the ESP32's 3.3 V (the eval board accepts 2.25 to 3.6 V), over I²C at 0x1D (ASEL low) or 0x53 (ASEL high), and the appliance pack asks for 2000 Hz. Its own range and noise are in its datasheet: check the datasheet.

| Pack | Head | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| footstep-id | geophone | velocity of a step travelling through the floor |
| fall | geophone | the same hardware; an impact is much bigger, so gain and clipping matter |
| appliance | accelerometer | machines vibrate at higher frequencies where acceleration is large; second head type |
| none (test only) | load cell | known signal for the whole chain |
| later | piezo | needs clamp + buffer; and a 3rd or 4th analog channel, so the ADS131M04 PCB |

## @datasheet
### SM-24 geophone

We do not have the manufacturer PDF in the repo. What we hold is the shop listing (Opencircuit: 28.8 V/(m/s), 10 to 240 Hz, 168 g with insulating disc) and secondary quotes (natural frequency 10 Hz ±2.5 %, coil resistance 375 Ω ±2.5 %, damping values above). The quantities to read in the real datasheet: *sensitivity* (open circuit), *natural frequency*, *coil resistance*, *damping vs shunt resistance*, and *distortion*. Confirm them before quoting any of them to a customer.

### YZC-133 load cell

Also only a shop listing, not a manufacturer datasheet: rated output 1.0 ±0.1 mV/V, zero balance ±0.1 mV/V, input resistance about 1066 Ω, output about 1000 Ω, excitation 3 to 10 V. Notice that our bridge runs at about 2.0 V, **below** the listed excitation range. That is fine for a test signal, but it is not a calibrated use, so don't trust absolute kilograms.

### ADXL355 (Analog Devices ADXL354/ADXL355)

Verified from the datasheet text (in the part note):
- Pin 2 SCLK/VSSIO: "Connect to VSSIO for I2C". Pin 1 CS/SCL clock, pin 3 MOSI/SDA data, pin 4 MISO/ASEL address select.
- The chip detects I²C or SPI by itself. Address 0x1D with ASEL low, 0x53 with ASEL high.
- Bus speeds 100 kHz, 400 kHz, 1 MHz, 3.4 MHz. Pull-ups on SDA/SCL are needed (about 2.2 to 4.7 kΩ); whether our board has them is not known.
- Tip: the datasheet writes "I²C" with a superscript, so searching "I2C" finds nothing. Search "SCL", "ASEL" or "VSSIO".

### What the ADS131M0x datasheet says about inputs

"Absolute Maximum Ratings": inputs survive only AGND −1.6 V to AVDD +0.3 V. At gain ≥ 8, keep inputs between AGND −1.3 V and AVDD −1.8 V. That is why a piezo head, which can swing beyond both limits, needs a series resistor, clamp diodes and a buffer. A geophone sits near zero volts and is a safe, passive input.
