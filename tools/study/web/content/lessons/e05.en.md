## @intuition
### Four questions, four ways to ask

A meter asks the circuit one of four questions. Each needs a different connection.

| Question | Mode | How it's connected | Safe when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| How much push between A and B? | V | across: touch both points, circuit untouched | circuit live |
| How hard is it to push current through this? | Ω | across the part, circuit **dead** | USB unplugged |
| Is there a wire between A and B? | continuity (beep) | across, circuit dead | USB unplugged |
| How much flows here? | A / mA | in **series**: you break the loop and put the meter in the gap | meter set up before power |

The pipe picture: a voltmeter is a pressure gauge tapped on two points. A resistance meter is a hand pump that pushes a little water of its own, so the pipe must be empty. An ammeter is a flow meter that you have to splice into the pipe.

### Why Ω needs a dead circuit

In Ω mode the meter pushes its own small current through the part and reads the voltage that causes. If the circuit has its own voltages (USB is plugged in) they add to the meter's push, and the reading is nonsense. It can also hurt the meter.

{{predict:0}}

### The mA-jack trap

An ammeter has to be in the way of the current without slowing it, so its resistance is tiny. That is fine in series with a load, and a disaster across a supply, where it's a near-short.

{{predict:1}}

> [!warn] Where the picture breaks. A voltmeter isn't perfectly "invisible": it has a finite input resistance and takes a tiny current. In our low-resistance circuits that doesn't matter; in high-resistance spots (megaohms) it does.

### How the 680 kΩ was found

1. **Voltage, USB on:** 3.3 V on the ESP end of the "680 Ω" and 0 V on the cell end. Two good readings, three suspects: open joint, short to GND, wrong resistor.
2. **KVL (E04):** the whole 3.3 V is dropping across the resistor, so it is open or much bigger than the bridge.
3. **Ω, USB unplugged:** the checks with the supply off separate the suspects. An open joint would read "OL" on every range. A short to GND would beep. The bridge itself read in the expected range, so the cell was fine.
4. **Bands:** blue, grey, **yellow**: 680 kΩ. Measuring it in Ω mode on the right range confirms it. The 200 mV range even showed the leftover ≈ 5 mV across the bridge, where the 20 V range showed 0.00.

Some of the readings in the case are reconstructed from the calculated values, not logged on the day; the order and the logic are the real ones.

> [!ok] Habit: when in doubt about a part, unplug the USB, lift one leg, set Ω, pick a range above the expected value, and read. It takes five seconds.

### Safe habits

- **After any current measurement**, the red lead goes back to V/Ω.
- **Before Ω or continuity**: USB unplugged, nothing live.
- **Start on the highest range** (or auto) when you don't know the size.
- Hold the probes by the insulation. This site covers 3.3 V electronics only; never measure mains with a hobby meter.

## @datasheet
### What your meter's manual says

Our repo doesn't hold the data of our meter, so check its manual for the numbers that matter here:

| Spec | Why we care |
| --- | --- |
| DC voltage ranges, accuracy and resolution | a 5 mV signal on a 20 V range rounds to nothing |
| Input resistance on the V range (often about 10 MΩ; check) | loading in high-resistance circuits |
| Ω range: open-circuit test voltage and current | too high a test voltage can disturb a protected input |
| mA / A jack: burden voltage, fuse rating | what happens if the jack goes across a supply |
| Safety category (CAT rating) | what the meter may be connected to |

### What our other datasheets say about shorting

- **ESP32-S3 and the DevKit:** the 3V3 pin comes from the board's regulator; shorting it to GND with a low-resistance path stresses that regulator. The current limit is in the DevKit and regulator datasheets (E17).
- **ADS131M0x "Absolute Maximum Ratings":** inputs AGND −1.6 V to AVDD +0.3 V; and at gain ≥ 8 AGND −1.3 V to AVDD −1.8 V. A meter probe on an ADC input can push charge into it; the Daily note recorded CH1 jumping whenever the probe touched Click CH1+. Probe the other side, or the screw terminal, with the power in a known state.
- **Continuity beep:** the beep threshold is a few tens of ohms in most meters; the exact value is in the manual.
