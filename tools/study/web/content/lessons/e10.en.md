## @bench
**D-016 in one question.** The ADC records at 2 kSPS, but the footstep-id and fall packs want 1 kSPS. The cheapest way to get there is to keep every second sample. The firmware does not do that: it runs a 63-tap [[fir|FIR]] filter first. Why does a filter have to come before throwing samples away?

By the end you can answer that, and you can design and read the simplest filter there is: an RC.

## @intuition
### A filter is a sieve for frequencies

A [[lowpass|low-pass filter]] lets slow movements through and holds fast ones back. Think of a heavy flywheel on a wobbling shaft: slow turns drag it along, fast jitters average out and it barely moves. Where it starts holding back is the [[cutoff-frequency|cut-off frequency]]. A [[highpass|high-pass]] does the reverse.

The analogy breaks at the edge: the flywheel fades out gradually, and so does a simple filter. A cut-off isn't a wall. How steep the fade is, is its [[roll-off]].

{{w:rc mode=filter r=1000 c=100e-9 f=1590}}

Slide the input frequency across the cut-off. Read the gain in dB at each point: that picture is a [[bode|Bode plot]].

### Why throwing samples away needs a filter first

Take a film of a spinning wheel with too few frames per second and the wheel seems to turn backwards. That is [[aliasing]]. A signal faster than half the sample rate (the [[nyquist]] frequency) doesn't vanish when you sample it: it **folds** to a false lower frequency.

When we drop every second sample, the rate halves and the Nyquist limit falls from 1000 Hz to 500 Hz. Anything between 500 and 1000 Hz was fine a moment ago and now folds into the band we care about.

{{predict:0}}

{{w:sampling f=700 fs=1000}}

### The sieve we use before dropping samples

The cure is an [[anti-aliasing|anti-aliasing filter]]: remove the troublemakers first. In our chain two filters do it, the ADC's own sinc filter and the firmware FIR. [[decimation]] means "filter, then keep every Nth".

{{w:decimate f=700 taps=63}}

Switch the filter off in the diagram to see the 700 Hz tone show up at 300 Hz. Then try 15, 31 and 63 taps: more taps give a sharper edge.

> [!warn] More taps are not free: more computing and a longer delay. Our 63 taps are a compromise we chose, not a law.

## @engineer
### RC filter

$$ fc = 1 / (2π · R · C)        |H| = 1 / √(1 + (f / fc)²) $$

{{calc:0}}

| f | |H| | dB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| fc / 10 | 0.995 | −0.04 |
| fc | 0.707 | −3.01 |
| 2 · fc | 0.447 | −6.99 |
| 10 · fc | 0.0995 | −20.04 |

{{calc:1}}

Above fc a single RC falls **20 dB per decade**. Swap R and C (output across R) and you get a high-pass with the same fc. Two RC stages in series fall twice as fast but also bend the corner.

### Where an analog RC would go

The ADC's modulator samples very fast internally, so the ADC's own digital filter (D-016 uses its response) does the first anti-aliasing. Whether we also add an analog RC in front of each input on our PCB is **not decided**. If we do, it joins the clamp resistor from E11: R and the input capacitance already make a low-pass.

### The FIR

A FIR filter computes every output as a weighted sum of the last N inputs:

$$ y[n] = Σ h[k] · x[n − k],   k = 0 … N−1 $$

The weights h[k] are the "taps". Ours (from `dsp.py`, copied verbatim into `rz_decim.c`):

| Property | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Taps | 63 |
| Design | Parks-McClellan (scipy `remez`), equal weights |
| Input / output rate | 2000 SPS → 1000 SPS (keep odd raw indices) |
| Pass band | 0–400 Hz |
| Stop band | 600–1000 Hz |
| Delay | 31 raw samples |

{{calc:2}}

The weights are symmetric, so the delay is the same for every frequency (linear [[phase]]). That is why the shape of a footstep isn't distorted, only shifted in time.

{{predict:1}}

### Lost samples

If a raw sample is lost, firmware holds the previous value and marks every output whose 63-sample window touched it as invalid. It counts them. Nothing is hidden.

### Pack filters (a different filter)

A pack that asks for `highpass_hz` or `lowpass_hz` gets a 2nd-order Butterworth biquad (−3.01 dB at that frequency). The schema only gives the cut-off, so family and order are our convention, noted in the code as an open question for Viktor. Don't confuse it with the decimation FIR.

### The ADC's own filter

The ADS131M0x datasheet describes a digital filter response. Check the section on the digital filter before relying on any figure from this lesson: the ADC's response is given for its own data rate, and it is scaled by the clock. Our clock runs about −0.21 % low, so the true rate is 1995.80 Hz, not 2000 Hz. The sidecar records the measured rate.
