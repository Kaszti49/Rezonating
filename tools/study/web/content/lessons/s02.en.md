## @intuition
### Three ways to describe the same shake

Take a child on a swing, or a floor board that bounces a little.

- **[[displacement|Displacement]]**: *where* is it? How far from rest (micrometres for a floor).
- **[[velocity|Velocity]]**: *how fast* is it moving right now? Fastest as it passes the middle.
- **[[acceleration|Acceleration]]**: *how hard is it being pushed*? Largest at the ends, where it turns around.

For a smooth back-and-forth shake they are the same wave, shifted in time. The key is the [[frequency]]: the faster it shakes, the more a given movement means a big velocity and an even bigger push.

{{predict:0}}

> [!warn] An analogy limit: the swing has one frequency. A real floor shakes at many frequencies at once, and each one follows this rule on its own.

### A footstep is a knock, not a hum

A heel hits the floor: the board is pushed suddenly, rings for a moment like a tapped table, and goes quiet until the next step. That is a **[[burst]]**. At a normal pace there are roughly two steps a second (typical, not measured by us). Where its energy sits in frequency is an *expectation*: low frequencies, roughly 10 Hz up to a few hundred Hz. We will check this with the first geophone recordings.

### The floor is part of the sensor

Concrete is stiff and ends the ringing quickly; wooden joists bounce and ring longer. The same person, the same step, makes a different signal. A pack trained on one floor has to be shown the floor it will work on. That is why the Year 1 target says "3 people on our own floor".

### A fall and a machine, as signals

- **A fall**: one hard impact, much stronger than a step, followed by no steps for a while. The fall pack uses a threshold trigger, 500 ms before and 3000 ms after, and the rule "no footsteps for 20 s afterwards".
- **A machine** (washer, fridge, pump): not a knock but a *repeating* pattern, at frequencies set by its motor. The appliance pack learns what is normal for each state and flags drift.

These two descriptions are expectations from the pack notes. We have no recordings of them yet.

## @engineer
### The conversion

For a motion of one frequency *f*:

$$ v = 2π · f · x        a = 2π · f · v = (2π · f)² · x $$

Keep metres and seconds. Starting from velocity (what the SM-24 gives):

$$ x = v / (2π · f)        a = 2π · f · v $$

### One velocity, four frequencies

For v = 1 mm/s (calculated with the formulas above):

| f | Displacement | Acceleration | In g |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 10 Hz | 15.9 µm | 0.063 m/s² | 0.0064 |
| 50 Hz | 3.18 µm | 0.314 m/s² | 0.032 |
| 100 Hz | 1.59 µm | 0.628 m/s² | 0.064 |
| 200 Hz | 0.80 µm | 1.257 m/s² | 0.128 |

Read it twice: the same velocity means *less* movement and *more* push as the frequency rises. A displacement sensor would favour low frequencies, an accelerometer high ones, a geophone sits in the middle. The head you pick tilts the spectrum the pack sees.

### The numbers the packs use

| Pack | High-pass | Low-pass | Trigger | Window |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| footstep-id | 5 Hz | 400 Hz | STA/LTA 50 ms / 2000 ms, ratio 3.5 | 100 ms before, 400 ms after |
| fall | 2 Hz | 400 Hz | threshold 0.0005, units not yet defined in the manifest | 500 ms before, 3000 ms after |
| appliance | 3 Hz | none | every 10 s | baseline of 5 cycles, alert at 4 σ |

All values are from the version 0.0.1 manifests and are starting points. The high-pass removes slow drift and building sway; the low-pass at 400 Hz goes with the 1 kSPS rate.

{{calc:2}}

### Why the sample rate follows from the signal

If the interesting energy ends near a few hundred Hz, the Nyquist rule says you need more than twice that: 1 kSPS holds up to 500 Hz. The appliance pack asks for 2000 Hz because machine vibration can contain higher frequencies. That is a design choice recorded in the pack manifest, not something we have measured.

## @datasheet
This lesson is about physics, not one chip, so the "datasheet" here is what the datasheets *say about units*.

### Where the units come from

| Part | What the datasheet quotes | In this lesson's terms |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SM-24 geophone | sensitivity 28.8 V/(m/s) (shop listing: 10–240 Hz, 10 Hz natural frequency) | velocity |
| ADXL355 | output in g, with selectable ranges and a noise density per axis: check the datasheet | acceleration |
| YZC-133 | mV/V at the rated load (shop listing, not a manufacturer datasheet) | force, not a floor motion |

Converting a datasheet number: 1 g = 9.81 m/s². The ADXL355's own range, resolution and noise numbers are in its datasheet (Analog Devices ADXL354/ADXL355, section on specifications); we do not quote them here because the repo does not hold them.

### Reading the geophone's passband honestly

The SM-24 listing says 10–240 Hz. The geophone's own mechanical resonance is at 10 Hz, and below it the output falls off; that behaviour is S03. For the footstep pack the practical point is that the lowest part of the step spectrum may be weakened by the sensor, and that the 5 Hz high-pass in the manifest is not the only filter in the path.

### What is not yet known

- The real frequency content of a step on our floor.
- The real amplitude of a step in mm/s at the sensor.
- How a fall differs from a dropped object in our data.

All three will come from the first recordings, which is what the pack experiments are for.
