## @bench
**Next on the bench.** The ADXL355 board talks [[i2c|I²C]]: two wires, SDA and SCL, shared with every other chip on the head. If a scan finds nothing, you need to know what a healthy bus looks like at idle, who holds a line high, and who may pull it low. The ADC 15 Click, meanwhile, is 3.3 V logic only: a 5 V signal on its pins is a fault.

By the end you can read "3.3 V" as a pair of thresholds, size a transistor switch, and explain why I²C was built open-drain.

## @intuition
### A transistor is a tap that another wire turns

A [[bjt|BJT]] and a [[mosfet|MOSFET]] both let a small signal control a big current. Think of a tap on a water pipe.

- **BJT:** you must keep a small trickle running into the control wire (the base) to hold the tap open. Small trickle in, big flow out.
- **MOSFET:** the control wire (the gate) is insulated. A voltage on it opens the tap and almost no current flows in. Charging the gate takes a moment, then it just holds.

Where the analogy breaks: a transistor used as a *switch* must be pushed fully open or fully shut. In the half-open zone it carries current *and* drops voltage, so it gets hot.

### What "high" and "low" mean

A [[logic-level|logic level]] is not one voltage but two zones with a gap between them. On 3.3 V CMOS, anything under about a third of the supply reads as 0, anything over about two thirds reads as 1, and the middle is **undefined**. The gap gives noise room to do no harm. It is also why a floating input is a bug: it sits in the middle and reads whatever the noise says.

### Two kinds of output

A [[push-pull]] output has a switch to 3.3 V and a switch to ground, so it drives both levels firmly. An [[open-drain]] output has only the switch to ground. The high level comes from a [[pull-up|pull-up resistor]] when the switch lets go.

{{predict:0}}

That is why I²C uses open-drain: any chip may pull the shared wire low, nobody ever drives it high, so nobody can fight. The price is speed. Low is fast (an active switch). High is slow (a resistor charging the wire's capacitance).

## @engineer
### BJT as a switch

Drive the base through a resistor. The load sets the load current; the base current only has to be large enough to saturate the transistor. A safe habit is a **forced gain of 10**:

$$ I_B = I_load / 10        R_B = (V_drive − V_BE) / I_B $$

V_BE ≈ 0.7 V for silicon (textbook value).

{{calc:0}}

Inductive loads (a relay, a motor) need a diode across them: the coil pushes a voltage spike when you switch it off.

### MOSFET as a switch

N-channel, low-side: source to ground, load between the supply and the drain, GPIO to the gate (usually via a small series resistor, plus a pull-down so the gate is never floating at power-up). Two numbers matter at *your* gate voltage:

| Parameter | Meaning | What you want from a 3.3 V pin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V_GS(th) | gate voltage where it *starts* to conduct | well below 3.3 V |
| [[rds-on|R_DS(on)]] | resistance when fully on, **at a stated V_GS** | low, and specified at 3.3 V or lower |

Loss in the switch is P = I² · R_DS(on). Pick a *logic-level* MOSFET.

### Logic levels on our bench

| Item | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Supply of the ESP32 pins and the ADC 15 Click | 3.3 V |
| Input reads low | ≤ 0.3 · 3.3 V ≈ 1.0 V |
| Input reads high | ≥ 0.7 · 3.3 V ≈ 2.3 V |
| 5 V logic into these pins | not allowed |

The thresholds above are the usual [[vih-vil|V_IL / V_IH]] CMOS rule. The exact limits and the pin current limits are in the ESP32 datasheet's DC characteristics.

### I²C pull-ups

The bus is a capacitor charged through a resistor, so the rise time is

$$ t_r ≈ 0.8473 · R_pullup · C_bus $$

The other limit is the low level: the chip pulls the line down against the pull-up current.

| Pull-up | Rise time at 100 pF | Current while low |

| 4.7 kΩ | 398 ns | 0.70 mA |

## @datasheet
### ESP32 / ESP32-S3 datasheet

Open **DC Characteristics**: V_IL, V_IH, V_OL, V_OH and the per-pin output current. They give the real thresholds and how much a pin may source or sink. **Absolute Maximum Ratings** gives the highest voltage a pin survives. We do not quote the numbers here because they differ between the classic ESP32 and the S3.

### ADXL355 (Analog Devices datasheet, pin descriptions)

The chip picks SPI or I²C by itself. Pin 2, SCLK/VSSIO, must be connected to VSSIO (ground) for I²C. Pin 1 is SCL, pin 3 is SDA, and pin 4 (ASEL) selects the address: 0x1D with ASEL low, 0x53 with ASEL high. Pull-ups on SDA and SCL are needed; whether our breakout has them is not known yet (part note).

### The I²C specification (NXP UM10204)

Standard mode (100 kHz) allows a rise time up to 1000 ns, fast mode (400 kHz) up to 300 ns. The low level must stay under 0.3 · VDD at a specified sink current. The spec also limits total bus capacitance (400 pF). These are textbook/spec values, not measured on our rig.

### A MOSFET datasheet, section by section

1. **Absolute Maximum Ratings:** V_DS, V_GS, continuous drain current.
2. **Electrical Characteristics:** V_GS(th) with min and max, and R_DS(on) at several V_GS values. Use the row at 3.3 V.
3. **Gate charge (Q_g):** how much charge the GPIO must push in; matters for fast switching.
