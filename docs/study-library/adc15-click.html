The ADC 15 Click and direct wiring

What changed in the first prototype on 2026-10-06, and how the new parts work. Read it after the study guide. The same source tags apply: datasheet repo calculated assumed.

What changed (2026-10-06)

  • ADC: the hand-soldered ADS131M04 on a TSSOP-20 adapter and the separate 8.192 MHz oscillator are replaced by a ready-made MikroElektronika ADC 15 Click (MIKROE-4890), wired directly to the ESP32-S3.
  • Channels: the Click has an ADS131M02, so the prototype has 2 analog channels (two geophones), plus the ADXL355 on I²C. A third or fourth analog head (for example piezo) waits for our own PCB.
  • Clock: it now comes from an SPI-programmable oscillator chip (LTC6903) that firmware must set before the ADC answers.
  • Unchanged: the 4-channel ADS131M04 stays the target for the PCB. So do the sensors, the microSD card and the packs.
  • Status: the choice is a proposal recorded on 2026-10-06. The board is not in the lab yet, so nothing here has been measured on it.

What changed and why

We stopped building the ADC and bought one. The cost is two analog channels instead of four.

The two hardest jobs on the old plan were soldering a 0.65 mm pitch chip and getting a clean, continuous 8.192 MHz clock to it. The Click removes both. A ready-made board also has a proper layout around the chip, which a breadboard cannot match.

What the node can do now

  • Two SM-24 geophones sampled at the same instant. That is enough for footstep-id and fall, which use one geophone head.
  • The ADXL355 on I²C for appliance. It does not use the ADC.
  • WAV and sidecar recording to microSD, as before.

What it cannot do until our PCB

  • Read a third or fourth analog head, for example a piezo disc. The M02 has two channels. A piezo head would also need its buffer and clamp (see the sensors section of the guide).

The PCB goes back to the 4-channel ADS131M04, so the four-head design is not given up. Only the first rig is smaller.

Say it to a judge

For the first rig we bought a ready-made two-channel ADC board instead of hand-soldering a four-channel chip. It saves weeks of risky soldering and still covers our two geophones and the accelerometer. The four-channel chip comes back on our own board.

Self-check

1. Which two hard jobs does the Click remove?

Soldering the TSSOP-20 chip (0.65 mm pitch) and providing a clean continuous 8.192 MHz clock.

2. What can the new prototype not do?

Read a third or fourth analog head such as a piezo. It has 2 analog channels. The ADXL355 is digital and is unaffected.

3. Does the ADC 15 Click replace the ADS131M04 for good?

No. It is for the prototype. The 4-channel ADS131M04 stays the target for our PCB.

Both chips run from 250 SPS (OSR 16384) to 64 kSPS (OSR 64, turbo) with an 8.192 MHz clock, per their datasheets. (Older project notes said 500 SPS for the M04; the datasheet says 250.) For our use, 1 to 4 kSPS is far from either limit.

Footnote: the M02 datasheet is not consistent here. Its Table 8-2 prints 32 kSPS for the high-resolution OSR-64 row and section 8.3.7 says "as high as 32 kSPS", while its Features list and the M04 table say 64 kSPS (4.096 MHz / 64). Our 1 to 4 kSPS is unaffected.

Full scale at gain 32

Worked example

FSR = ±1.2 V / 32 = ±0.0375 V = ±37.5 mV1 LSB = 2 × 37.5 mV / 224 ≈ 4.5 nV

The SM-24 gives 28.8 V per m/s. So ±37.5 mV is about ±1.3 mm/s of floor velocity at the coil. The 1 kΩ shunt passes about 0.73 of the open-circuit voltage (from the guide), so the same ±37.5 mV at the input is about ±1.8 mm/s of floor velocity. calculated

All three rows come from the ADS131M02 datasheet, Recommended Operating Conditions and Absolute Maximum Ratings datasheet. Beyond the absolute maximum the chip can be damaged. The recommended range is where it measures correctly.

Why tying the coil's negative side to AGND is fine

Each input pin may sit anywhere in the range above. A geophone coil produces millivolts. If one end of the coil goes to AGND (0 V), the other end swings a few millivolts either side of 0 V. That is well inside AGND − 1.3 V to AVDD − 1.8 V. Whether the Click's input connector allows this connection has still to be checked on its schematic.

Same anti-aliasing issue

The guide shows that the ADC's sinc filter attenuates only 4.4 dB at Nyquist at 1 kSPS. The M02 has the same kind of filter, so the same problem applies. The proposal stands: record at 4 kSPS and decimate to 1 kSPS in firmware with a sharp FIR filter. It is a proposal, not yet measured.

Say it to a judge

The ADS131M02 is the two-channel sibling of the four-channel chip we plan for our PCB. It samples both channels at the same instant with 24 bits. At gain 32 its full scale is plus or minus 37.5 millivolts, which suits a geophone.

Self-check

1. What is the full scale at gain 32?

±1.2 V / 32 = ±37.5 mV.

2. What is the recommended input range at gain 8 to 128?

AGND − 1.3 V to AVDD − 1.8 V, which is about +1.5 V at the top with a 3.3 V supply. At gain 1 to 4 the top is AVDD.

3. Why can the coil's negative side go to AGND?

The coil signal is millivolts around 0 V, and AGND is well inside the allowed input range at every gain.

4. How many words are in an M02 frame, and how many in an M04 frame?

M02: response, 2 channel words and CRC = 4 words. M04: response, 4 channel words and CRC = 6 words.

5. Does the anti-aliasing plan change for the M02?

No. Record at 4 kSPS and decimate in firmware, exactly as for the M04.

The board's clock

The ADC needs a continuous clock on CLKIN. On the Click that clock is made by a chip we program over SPI, and it is slightly off 8.192 MHz.

How a programmable oscillator works

A crystal oscillator rings at one frequency set by the cut of the crystal. A silicon oscillator has no crystal. It has an internal oscillator, and the frequency you get depends on settings you write into it. In principle, an internal source is divided by a power of two (the octave setting) and fine-tuned by a digital-to-analog converter setting (the DAC value). Firmware writes both numbers in one 16-bit word, and the output frequency follows.

The Mikroe driver asks for 8 192 000 Hz (ADC15_FREQ_MODE_HIGH_RESOLUTION), which works out to OCT 12, DAC 984. That gives 8 191 507 Hz, about −60 ppm from 8.192 MHz driver. This is only the rounding error of the setting; the chip's own accuracy is larger (see the table below). The ADC accepts a clock of 0.3 to 8.4 MHz in high-resolution mode with 40 to 60 % duty datasheet, so 8.1915 MHz (8 191 507 Hz) is inside the allowed range.

Worked example: the setting and the error

f = 2OCT × 2078 Hz / (2 − DAC/1024)OCT = floor(3.322 × log10(f / 1039)) = 12DAC = round(2048 − 2078 × 210+OCT / f) = round(984.06) = 984f = 4096 × 2078 / (2 − 984/1024) = 8 191 507 Hzerror = (8 191 507 − 8 192 000) / 8 192 000 ≈ −60 ppm

The formula and the OCT table (OCT 12 covers 4.256 to 8.503 MHz) are from the LTC6903 datasheet. datasheet The 16-bit word is OCT in bits D15 to D12, DAC in D11 to D2 and two CNF bits in D1 to D0 (they set the output configuration). It is sent most significant bit first, and the chip reads SDI on rising SCK edges while SEN is low, which fits SPI mode 0. datasheet

Why it matters to us

The ADC's sample rate is the clock divided by a fixed number. If the clock is a given amount low, every sample rate is the same amount low. Nothing breaks, but the real rate is not exactly the nominal one. Time-based features, such as a frequency scale or the stride interval of footsteps, use the sample rate. And "count every sample" means we should know the true rate, not assume it.

Worked example: clock error at 1 kSPS over one hour

samples in 1 h = 1000 × 3600 = 3 600 000setting rounding, 60 ppm: 3 600 000 × 60 × 10-6 = 216 sampleschip accuracy, 0.5 % typ: 3 600 000 × 0.005 = 18 000 sampleschip accuracy, 1.1 % max: 3 600 000 × 0.011 = 39 600 samples

The 60 ppm rounding alone is 216 samples an hour (0.216 s). The chip's own accuracy dominates: typically about 18 000 samples (18 s), at worst about 39 600. Over a short footstep window the effect is small. Over an hour of timestamps against the wall clock it adds up. Even +1.6 % (about 8.32 MHz) stays inside the ADC's 0.3 to 8.4 MHz CLKIN range. calculated

What we do about it

  1. Count the DRDY pulses over a known time against the ESP32-S3 timer. The ratio is the measured sample rate.
  2. Write the measured rate into the sidecar next to the nominal one.
  3. Keep counting dropped samples separately. A slow clock is not a drop.

The ESP32's timer is itself a crystal-based clock, so this measures the ADC against a better reference. Its own accuracy is not stated here.

Say it to a judge

The Click makes its clock with a programmable chip, not a crystal, so the real sample rate can be off by up to about 1 % (0.5 % typical) from the nominal value. That is why we measure it. We measure it by counting data-ready pulses against the microcontroller timer and store the measured rate with every recording.

Self-check

1. How does a programmable oscillator choose its frequency?

An internal oscillator is divided by a power of two (octave) and fine-tuned by a DAC value. Both come from a word written over SPI.

2. What does the Mikroe driver set, and what is the error?

OCT 12 and DAC 984, 8 191 507 Hz, about −60 ppm. That is only the setting's rounding error; the chip itself is 0.5 % typical, 1.1 % max.

3. How many samples off in one hour at 1 kSPS with 60 ppm error, and with 0.5 %?

3 600 000 × 60 × 10-6 = 216 samples; 3 600 000 × 0.005 = 18 000 samples.

4. How do we measure the real sample rate?

Count DRDY pulses against the ESP32 timer over a known time, and write the result into the sidecar.

SPI with two devices and two modes

Two chips share one SPI bus. They need different modes and different chip-select wires.

ADS131M02 (ADC)LTC6903 (clock)
SPI mode1 (CPOL 0, CPHA 1)0 (CPOL 0, CPHA 0)
Chip selectmikroBUS CSCS2 = mikroBUS PWM pin
TrafficFrames of 24-bit words by default (16/24/32 selectable via WLENGTH); a default M02 frame is 4 × 24 = 96 bits datasheetOne 16-bit word, written only
Driver speed1 MHz (Mikroe driver)Not stated here

The modes, the pins and the 1 MHz are from the Mikroe driver driver.

What the modes mean

The clock line (SCLK) idles low in both modes (CPOL 0). The difference is when the receiver reads each bit. In mode 0 (CPHA 0) the bit is stable before the first rising edge and is read on the rising edge. In mode 1 (CPHA 1) the sender changes the bit on the rising edge and the receiver reads it on the falling edge. Use the wrong mode and every bit is read half a clock off, which gives garbage.

On ESP-IDF

One SPI bus is initialised once. Two device handles are added to it, each with its own mode, chip-select pin and clock speed. The driver then sets the right mode for each transaction.

// sketch, not tested code; pin numbers not decided yet
spi_bus_initialize(host, &bus_cfg, SPI_DMA_CH_AUTO);

spi_device_interface_config_t adc = { .mode = 1, .clock_speed_hz = 1000000, .spics_io_num = PIN_CS, .queue_size = 1 };
spi_device_interface_config_t osc = { .mode = 0, .clock_speed_hz = 1000000, .spics_io_num = PIN_CS2, .queue_size = 1 };
spi_bus_add_device(host, &adc, &adc_handle);
spi_bus_add_device(host, &osc, &osc_handle);

The microSD breakout can share the same SCK, MISO and MOSI lines as a third device with its own chip select. This shared bus is not yet tested with the Click.

Start-up order

  1. Power on. 3.3 V to the Click.
  2. Program the LTC6903. It already runs at power-up, but at the wrong frequency: its registers reset to 0, which gives 1.039 kHz, far below the ADC's 0.3 MHz minimum CLKIN. Write the 16-bit word over SPI mode 0 on CS2.
  3. The clock runs. CLKIN now has about 8.19 MHz. The ADC cannot answer before this.
  4. Reset the ADC. Pulse RST low for 1 ms, then wait 100 ms (as in the Mikroe default configuration).
  5. Set word length and gains. Then read frames in mode 1.
  6. Wait for DRDY. DRDY is active low: wait for it to go low (a falling edge on INT) and discard the first two samples after start-up datasheet. Firmware uses a falling-edge interrupt.

Say it to a judge

Two chips share one SPI bus, but they use different SPI modes and different chip-select wires. The firmware first programs the clock chip, then resets the ADC, then waits for the data-ready line.

Self-check

1. Which SPI mode does each chip use?

ADS131M02: mode 1 (CPOL 0, CPHA 1). LTC6903: mode 0.

2. Which wire is the LTC6903's chip select?

CS2, which is the mikroBUS PWM pin.

3. What is the difference between mode 0 and mode 1 on the data line?

In mode 0 the receiver reads on the rising edge. In mode 1 it reads on the falling edge, half a clock later.

4. How do you serve two modes on ESP-IDF?

Add two spi_device handles to one bus, each with its own mode and chip-select pin.

5. Why must the oscillator be programmed before the ADC reset?

The ADC needs a running clock on CLKIN to respond at all. No clock, no reply, no DRDY.

mikroBUS and direct wiring

The Click is made for a mikroBUS socket. We have no socket on the ESP32-S3 board, so we wire to the Click's pins directly with short wires.

What mikroBUS is

mikroBUS is a standard from MikroElektronika: two rows of 8 pins that put the same signals on the same pins for every Click board standard.

Do not use GPIO 33 to 37

The ESP32-S3-N16R8 has octal PSRAM, which takes GPIO 33 to 37 (data lines 4 to 7 on GPIO 33–36 and the strobe on GPIO 37; ESP32-S3 datasheet, Table 2-14) datasheet. Pick the SPI, DRDY and RST pins from other free GPIOs.

The ADXL355 on I²C

  • Wires: SCL, SDA, power and ground. The bus needs pull-up resistors on SCL and SDA. The values and whether the evaluation board already has them are to be confirmed to confirm.
  • The SCLK/VSSIO pin goes to ground to select I²C mode.
  • The ASEL pin sets the address: low gives 0x1D, high gives 0x53.

Wiring rules for short, quiet wires

  • Keep SPI wires short. The edges of a 1 MHz clock carry much higher frequencies. Long wires act as antennas and ring.
  • Put a ground wire next to SCK. It gives the clock current a short return path. Twist the clock with its ground if the wires are long.
  • Keep the analog geophone leads away from the SPI lines. Millivolt signals are easy to disturb. Twist each geophone's two wires together and route them on the other side of the board from SCK and MOSI.
  • One common ground. Click, ESP32-S3, ADXL355 and microSD breakout all share GND.

These are general good practice, not results measured on our rig.

Say it to a judge

We connect the Click to the ESP32-S3 with nine short wires: the SPI lines, the data-ready and reset lines, a second chip select for the clock chip, power and ground. The weak analog geophone wires are twisted and routed away from the digital lines.

Self-check

1. What is mikroBUS?

A standard 2 × 8 socket that puts the same signals on the same pins on every Click board.

2. Which nine connections do we make?

SCK, MISO (SDO), MOSI (SDI), CS, INT (DRDY), RST, PWM (CS2), 3.3 V and GND.

3. Which GPIOs must we not use, and why?

GPIO 33 to 37: the octal PSRAM uses them for data lines 4 to 7 and the strobe (ESP32-S3 datasheet, Table 2-14).

4. Why twist the geophone wires and route them away from SPI?

The signal is millivolts. Twisting cancels pick-up and distance reduces coupling from the clock and data edges.

5. How does the ADXL355 select I²C and its address?

SCLK/VSSIO to ground selects I²C. ASEL low gives 0x1D, ASEL high gives 0x53.

The updated signal chain

The path of a footstep is the same as in the guide. Only the ADC and its clock changed.

Say it to a judge

Two geophones go into a two-channel ADC board that samples both at the same instant. A programmable chip on that board makes its clock. The microcontroller reads the samples over SPI, reads the accelerometer separately over I²C, and stores everything on a microSD card with a description of the setup.

Self-check

1. What does the ESP32-S3 read from the Click, and over which wire does it know a sample is ready?

Frames of channel data over SPI. The INT pin (DRDY) tells it a new sample set is ready.

2. Where does the ADXL355 enter the chain?

Directly at the ESP32-S3 over I²C. It never goes through the ADC.

3. What extra number is stored in the sidecar because of the new clock?

The measured sample rate, next to the nominal rate.

New glossary terms

mikroBUS
MikroElektronika's standard 2 × 8 pin socket. The same signals sit on the same pins for every Click board.
Click board
A small add-on board built to plug into a mikroBUS socket.
ADS131M02
Texas Instruments 2-channel, 24-bit simultaneous-sampling delta-sigma ADC. The sibling of the ADS131M04.
LTC6903
A programmable silicon oscillator chip. Its frequency is set by a 16-bit word over SPI.
Silicon oscillator
An oscillator built from an on-chip circuit instead of a quartz crystal. Usually less accurate and less stable than a crystal.
Programmable oscillator
An oscillator whose output frequency is chosen by digital settings, such as an octave divider and a DAC value.
Octave (OCT)
A setting that divides the frequency by powers of two in the LTC6903. The driver uses 12.
ppm
Parts per million. 60 ppm means an error of 60 in every 1 000 000, or 0.006 %.
CLKIN
The ADC's clock input. It needs a continuous clock; there is no internal oscillator.
LVCMOS
A common digital logic level standard. CLKIN takes an LVCMOS clock, which at 3.3 V swings between about 0 V and 3.3 V.
SPI mode
One of four combinations of clock idle level (CPOL) and the edge on which data is read (CPHA).
CPOL, CPHA
Clock polarity (idle level) and clock phase (which edge reads the data).
Chip select (CS)
A wire that tells one device on a shared SPI bus that the current transfer is for it.
DRDY
Data ready. A pin that signals a new sample set. On the Click it is the INT pin.
Pull-up resistor
A resistor from a bus line to the supply. I²C needs one on SCL and on SDA.
