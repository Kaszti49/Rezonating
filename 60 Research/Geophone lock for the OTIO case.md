# Geophone lock for the OTIO case

**Takeaway for Rezonating:** let the geophones stand on the floor, apart from the case, and lock them only for transport. Ocean-bottom sensors separate the sensor from the frame, and seismometers use a transport lock. Nothing found was measured on two small geophones indoors, so the gain has to be tested: same spots, cups against free-standing.

## Read
- **SM-24 datasheet** (I/O Sensor Nederland, 2006, text extracted from the PDF): Ø25.4 mm, height 32 mm, 74 g, moving mass 11 g, coil travel 2 mm peak to peak, maximum tilt 10° for the specified natural frequency, damping 0.69 with 1000 Ω. It doesn't show the body's shape, the bottom or the disc.
- **US 5983699** (Güralp, Google Patents, read in part): a camshaft lifts the boom until recesses seat on conical probes, three points of engagement; turning on releases it. No gap value found.
- **US 6061302** (Western Atlas, read): an electric clutch locks a sensor's gimbal. Not useful here.

## Search summary only, not opened
- US 10386520: an ocean-bottom node whose slack wires let the sensors settle on the seabed apart from the frame: https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10386520
- Isolated-sensor OBS (Hawaii, 1983): the separated sensor did better than the rigid package: https://ilse.kobv.de/KobvIndexRecord/nat_lic_papers_NLM195081536
- Flat geophone bases: transmission increases with weight and with a smaller base: https://khub.utp.edu.my/scholars/7152/ (site refused the connection)
- PLA sliding gaps: 0.15 to 0.5 mm depending on the source; holes print small.

## Not found
- A slip or lift-off limit for a free 74 g sensor on a hard floor (the OSMRE report was too large to fetch).
- Geophone-specific transport locks.
